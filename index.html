    How these are calculated

    Feels like. Shade, walking is the BOM formula AT = Ta + 0.33e − 0.70ws − 4.00 (e = vapour pressure in hPa, ws = 10 m wind in m/s). In sun adds the radiation term 0.70·Q/(ws+10) from BOM's radiation version, with Q = absorbed solar power per m² of skin (0.7 absorptivity, standing-person projected-area factor, half the sky diffuse). Ground reflection and hot-surface heat are ignored, so real sun can feel hotter still.

    Standing, running and body type are my additions, not BOM's. They shift the walking value by the change in metabolic heat per m² of skin: 0.06 K per W/m² below walking (standing ≈ 70 vs 135 W/m²), tapering logarithmically above it so running stays sensible. Running uses ~1 MET per km/h (Easy 9, Steady 12, Hard 15 km/h ≈ 400–640 W/m²) and adds running speed to the wind, which cools and slightly reduces the sun term. Body type scales metabolic heat by mass ÷ surface area (DuBois) relative to a 70 kg, 1.75 m adult. Indicative only: for running, the sports heat risk below is the better guide to actual danger.

    Hourly. Open-Meteo model data: past days are model analyses (close to, but not, station observations), later days are forecasts. Feels-like lines use the walking values for the selected body type.

    UV index. Open-Meteo hourly forecast (clear-sky-adjusted model UV). Categories follow the WHO scale; the protection window is the hours with UV ≥ 3, the SunSmart / BOM threshold.

    Excess Heat Factor. EHF = max(0, EHIsig) × max(1, EHIaccl) (Nairn & Fawcett) using daily mean temperature (max + next-day min)/2: EHIsig is the 3-day mean minus the local 95th percentile (T95), EHIaccl the 3-day mean minus the prior 30-day mean. T95 and EHF85 come from ERA5 reanalysis for 1971–2000 at your location, not a station record, so they differ a little from BOM's. Severe ≥ EHF85, extreme ≥ 3×EHF85. Recent days are model analyses, later days forecasts.

    Sports heat risk. The Sports Heat Tool is Sports Medicine Australia / University of Sydney's (sma-heat-policy.sydney.edu.au), not BOM's, and runs the full ISO 7933 heat-strain model. This page is a simplified steady-state heat balance using the same published sport inputs (clothing, activity, air speed, duration) and risk thresholds (sweat 850 mL/h; core 39.5° or 40°, 38.5° for low-intensity sports). It tracks the official tool in direction but not exactly: use the official tool for decisions.

    Thermal Work Limit. Brake & Bates' limiting metabolic rate: the highest work rate (W/m²) an acclimatised, hydrated, self-paced worker can sustain with core ≤ 38.2° and sweat ≤ 1.2 kg/h, standard 0.35 clo wet clothing. The full commercial algorithm is not published, so this is my reimplementation of its heat-balance structure with a radiant load estimated from the sun. Zones: <115 restricted, 115–140 buffer, 140–220 acclimatisation, >220 unrestricted. Not for compliance use.

    Weather: model data from Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0), not station observations. Ground-level wind is taken as 0.75 × the 10 m wind for sport and TWL.
